Footnotes to Plato

A passage-level citation index of five ancient authors across the scholarly journals

Welcome! Footnotes to Plato (and Others) is a tool for scholars in ancient philosophy, classics, theology, reception studies, intellectual history, history of the humanities, and digital humanities.

It tracks citations to five ancient authors—Homer, Pindar, Plato, Aristotle, and Paul—across a corpus of 48 academic journals, published between 1827 and 2022. Shoutout to the folks at JSTOR who kindly provided the data!

Three visualization tools let you analyze how scholarly attention distributes, between and within texts, at passage-level granularity, how that shifts over time, and how it differs from journal to journal and discipline to discipline. You can also create bibliographies of articles citing specific passages at one click.

For the list of journals covered see the methods page; for the full method see methods, for the data, the data page, and for contacting me with suggestions & questions see contact.

Three caveats, before you start

Select journals ≠ the whole field

The data only contains journal articles only — no monographs, edited volumes or commentaries, although book reviews are included, giving a small window onto that world. The sample skews Anglophone.

Uneven across two centuries

Data for the early decades is thin. Most citations date to after 1980.

For Plato and Aristotle, every count is a floor

Many Stephanus/Bekker numbers are ambiguous within and between the two pagination systems. 80b fits Meno, Phaedo, Timaeus or Posterior Analytics at once. Where the surrounding text does not settle it, we decline to guess. The floors reflect what we could place with confidence.

What a floor looks likeeight texts of 88

Solid is what we could place with confidence. The fade is traffic we detected but could not attribute — shared among the texts a bare page number could belong to, so it is an upper limit, never a subtotal. A short solid bar with a long fade means "much of its traffic couldn't be placed," not "little studied."

Citing and contributing#

If this contributed to published work, please cite as: Kretz, David, Footnotes to Plato: A Passage-Level Citation Index for Ancient Texts, Version 2.0, date. The derived data is published for download so results can be checked or reused.
