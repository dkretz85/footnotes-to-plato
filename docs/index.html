Footnotes to Plato

A passage-level citation index of five ancient authors across the journal literature

Welcome! Footnotes to Plato (and Others) is a tool for scholars in ancient philosophy, classics, theology, reception studies, intellectual history, history of the humanities, and digital humanities.

It tracks citations to five ancient authors—Homer, Pindar, Plato, Aristotle, and Paul—across a corpus of 48 academic journals, published between 1827 and 2022. Shoutout to the folks at JSTOR who kindly provided the data!

Three visualization tools let you analyze how scholarly attention distributes:
• between texts, and text-groups, over time,
• within texts, at passage-level granularity,
• and how it differs from journal to journal and discipline to discipline.

You can also create exportable bibliographies of articles citing a specific passage at one click.

For the list of journals covered see here; for the full method see methods, and for the data, the data page. For suggestions, questions, feedback feel free to get in touch here: contact.

Three caveats, before you start

Select journals ≠ the whole field

Journal articles only, although included book reviews give a small window onto monographs. The selection skews Anglophone.

Uneven across two centuries

The early decades are thin. Most citations date to after 1980.

Every count is a floor

Especially relevant for Plato and Aristotle. A reference like 80b fits Meno, Phaedo, Timaeus or Posterior Analytics at once. Where the surrounding text does not settle it, the script does not guess. See the graph below for detectable, but unplaceable citation traffic.

What a floor looks likeeight texts of 88

Solid is what we could place with confidence. The fade is traffic we detected but could not attribute — shared among the texts a bare page number could belong to, so it is an upper limit, never a subtotal. A short solid bar with a long fade means "much of its traffic couldn't be placed," not "little studied."

Citing and contributing#

If this contributed to published work, please cite as: Kretz, David, Footnotes to Plato: A Passage-Level Citation Index for Ancient Texts, Version 2.0, date. The derived data is published for download so results can be checked or reused.

Publish or edit a journal you would like to see included? Please write to me! Corrections, methodological objections, and suggestions for additional features are also all very welcome.
