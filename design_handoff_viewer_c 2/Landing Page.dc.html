Footnotes to Plato (and Others)

A passage-level citation index of five ancient authors across {{ nJournals }} academic journals

Welcome! Footnotes to Plato (and Others) is a tool for scholars in ancient philosophy, classics, theology, reception studies, intellectual history, history of the humanities, and digital humanities.

It tracks citations to five ancient authors—Homer, Pindar, Plato, Aristotle, and Paul—across a corpus of {{ nJournals }} academic journals, published between {{ yearFrom }} and {{ yearTo }}. Shoutout to the folks at JSTOR who kindly provided the data!

Three visualization tools allow you, between them, to analyze (1) how scholarly attention distributes between texts, text-clusters, but also within text, at passage-level granularity, (2) how all this shifts over time, and (3) how it differs from journal to journal, discipline to discipline.

Below is a short introduction to the three visualization tools, and some important-to-know caveats to keep in mind as you use these tools.

For the list of journals covered see here; for the full method & technical implementation details see here; for downloading the data see here; for contacting me to suggest further features, or ask questions, see here.

Happy researching!

Citing and contributing

If this contributed to published work, please cite as: Kretz, David, Footnotes to Plato: A Passage-Level Citation Index for Ancient Texts, Version 2.0, date. The derived data is published for download so results can be checked or reused.

Publish or edit a journal you would like to see included? Please write to me! Corrections, methodological objections, and suggestions for additional features are also all very welcome.
